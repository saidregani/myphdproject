\begin{abstract}
This paper proposes a control strategy for a grid-connected 2~MW direct-drive permanent magnet
synchronous generator (PMSG) wind turbine with a back-to-back converter and an $L$ filter. The outer
loops are designed by differential flatness: the rotor speed is the flat output of the maximum power
point tracking (MPPT) loop, and the energy stored in the DC link and in the filter is the flat output of
the DC-link loop. The inner current loops of both converters use passivity-based control (PBC), which
keeps the workless coupling of the machine and filter models and injects damping; a storage function
proves their stability. An adaptive 6-10-2 neural network, updated online with dead-zone,
$\sigma$-modification and back-propagation, adds a compensation signal to the flat loops without
changing the nominal gains. Simulations under turbulent wind, parameter mismatch, a 10\,\% error on
the aerodynamic model and a 20\,\% grid voltage dip are compared with PI vector control. The proposed
control tracks the MPPT speed 3.8 times more accurately and limits the DC-link deviation during the dip
to 3.6~V, against 11.6~V with PI and power feedforward, while the neural compensation reduces the
integral absolute speed error by 38\,\%.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Wind energy is one of the main sources in the transition towards low-carbon electricity
generation~\cite{iea2024}; a record 117~GW of new wind capacity was installed worldwide in
2023~\cite{gwec2024}. Direct-drive PMSG turbines remove the gearbox, which reduces maintenance and
losses, and their full-scale back-to-back converter decouples the generator from the
grid~\cite{polinder2013,wu2011}; it must extract the maximum power, regulate the DC-link voltage and
inject a clean current.

These tasks are usually performed by cascaded PI loops in rotating frames~\cite{chinchilla2006,yazdani2010}.
The PMSG model is nonlinear and coupled, and its parameters vary with temperature and saturation.
Nonlinear controllers have therefore been studied for wind turbines and PMSGs, such as
backstepping~\cite{mechter2016}, predictive control~\cite{messadi2015} and passivity-based
control~\cite{takhi2020}.

Differential flatness~\cite{fliess1995} computes the control input directly from a desired trajectory
of a flat output; using the stored energy as the flat output gives a simple linear power balance for DC
buses~\cite{thounthong2010}. Passivity-based control~\cite{ortega1998,ortega2002} shapes the energy of
the closed loop and keeps the physical structure of the system. Both methods rely on a model, so model
errors leave residual tracking errors. Adaptive neural networks can learn such residual terms
online~\cite{lewis1999}; in~\cite{regani2026}, an adaptive neural compensation was added to a nominal
controller of a DFIG without changing its gains.

This paper combines these tools for the complete chain of a 2~MW direct-drive PMSG turbine. The
individual techniques are known; the contributions are their adaptation to this chain and the
resulting analysis:
\begin{itemize}
\item a DC-link flat output that includes the energy of the $L$ filter, so that the grid-side law
contains the measured generator power as an exact feedforward and the DC-link error dynamics is linear;
\item PBC laws for the currents of both converters and a cascade proof that the complete nominal
interconnection (flat outer loops and PBC inner loops) is exponentially stable;
\item the transfer of this compensation, developed in~\cite{regani2026} for the rotor currents of a
DFIG, to the two flat loops of a PMSG, with a learning signal taken along their control directions
and normalized update laws, for which a direct boundedness proof is given;
\item an evaluation against PI vector control with and without power feedforward, over several wind
realizations, initial conditions and adaptation settings, and with a switched converter model.
\end{itemize}
\section{Wind Turbine Modeling}
The aerodynamic power captured by the turbine is~\cite{heier2014}
\begin{equation}
P_w = \frac{1}{2}\rho\pi R^2 C_p(\lambda,\beta)\,v^3
\end{equation}
where $\rho$ is the air density, $R$ the rotor radius, $v$ the wind speed, $\beta$ the pitch angle and
$C_p$ the power coefficient, which depends on the tip-speed ratio
\begin{equation}
\lambda = \frac{\Omega R}{v}
\end{equation}
with $\Omega$ the rotor speed. The power coefficient is modeled by
\begin{equation}
C_p = 0.5176\Bigl(\frac{116}{\lambda_i} - 0.4\beta - 5\Bigr)e^{-21/\lambda_i} + 0.0068\lambda
\end{equation}
with $1/\lambda_i = 1/(\lambda+0.08\beta) - 0.035/(\beta^3+1)$. For $\beta=0$, its maximum
$C_{p,\max}=0.48$ is reached at $\lambda_{opt}=8.1$. The aerodynamic torque is $T_w = P_w/\Omega$, and
the direct-drive shaft is described by the one-mass model
\begin{equation}
J\frac{d\Omega}{dt} = T_w - T_e - B\,\Omega
\label{eq:shaft}
\end{equation}
where $J$ is the total inertia, $T_e$ the electromagnetic torque and $B$ the viscous friction
coefficient. With $R=40$~m and $\Omega_n=2.23$~rad/s, the rated wind speed is
$v_n=\Omega_nR/\lambda_{opt}\approx11$~m/s and the captured power at $v_n$ is about 2~MW.

\section{PMSG and Back-to-Back Converter Model}
\subsection{PMSG}
In the rotor $dq$ frame, with the $d$ axis aligned with the magnet flux, generator convention and
$L_d=L_q=L_s$, the PMSG is described by~\cite{wu2011}
\begin{align}
L_s\frac{di_{sd}}{dt} &= -R_s i_{sd} + \omega_e L_s i_{sq} - v_{sd}\\
L_s\frac{di_{sq}}{dt} &= -R_s i_{sq} - \omega_e L_s i_{sd} + \omega_e\psi_f - v_{sq}
\end{align}
where $\omega_e = p\,\Omega$, $p$ is the number of pole pairs and $\psi_f$ the magnet flux. The
electromagnetic torque is
\begin{equation}
T_e = \frac{3}{2}\,p\,\psi_f\, i_{sq}.
\end{equation}
With $\bv{i}_s=[i_{sd}\;i_{sq}]^T$, $\bv{v}_s=[v_{sd}\;v_{sq}]^T$, $\bv{e}_q=[0\;1]^T$ and the
skew-symmetric matrix $\bv{J}=\left[\begin{smallmatrix}0&1\\-1&0\end{smallmatrix}\right]$, the model reads
\begin{equation}
L_s\dot{\bv{i}}_s = -R_s\bv{i}_s + \omega_e L_s\bv{J}\,\bv{i}_s + \omega_e\psi_f\bv{e}_q - \bv{v}_s.
\label{eq:pmsg}
\end{equation}
Since $\bv{i}_s^T\bv{J}\,\bv{i}_s=0$, the coupling term $\omega_e L_s\bv{J}\bv{i}_s$ is workless: it
exchanges energy between the two axes without producing or dissipating it.

\subsection{Back-to-Back Converter, DC Link and Grid}
The two-level converters are represented by their average model. The voltages they synthesize are
limited by the space-vector modulation range, $\|\bv{v}_s\|,\|\bv{v}_i\|\le V_{dc}/\sqrt{3}$, and,
neglecting losses, the DC-link capacitor obeys
\begin{equation}
C V_{dc}\frac{dV_{dc}}{dt} = P_{msc} - P_{gsc}
\label{eq:dc}
\end{equation}
with $P_{msc} = \frac{3}{2}\bv{v}_s^T\bv{i}_s$ and $P_{gsc} = \frac{3}{2}\bv{v}_i^T\bv{i}_g$. In the
grid-voltage-oriented frame ($v_{gd}=V_m$, $v_{gq}=0$), the $L$ filter gives
\begin{equation}
L_f\dot{\bv{i}}_g = -R_f\bv{i}_g + \omega_g L_f\bv{J}\,\bv{i}_g + \bv{v}_i - \bv{v}_g
\label{eq:filter}
\end{equation}
and the powers injected into the grid are $P = \frac{3}{2}v_{gd}i_{gd}$ and
$Q = -\frac{3}{2}v_{gd}i_{gq}$.

\subsection{Complete Model}
With the state $\bv{x}=[i_{sd}\;i_{sq}\;\Omega\;V_{dc}\;i_{gd}\;i_{gq}]^T$, the inputs
$\bv{u}=[\bv{v}_s^T\;\bv{v}_i^T]^T$ and the disturbances $v$ and $\bv{v}_g$, (\ref{eq:shaft}),
(\ref{eq:pmsg}), (\ref{eq:dc}) and (\ref{eq:filter}) form a sixth-order nonlinear model, denoted
$\dot{\bv{x}}=f(\bv{x},\bv{u},v,\bv{v}_g)$ in the following.
The parameters are listed in Table~\ref{tab:param}. The turbine data correspond to a standard 2~MW
turbine; the generator, DC-link, filter and grid data are those of the non-salient PMSG
of~\cite{hasan2021}. The magnet flux is consistent with the grid: at 9.75~Hz, the rated frequency
of~\cite{hasan2021}, the back-EMF $\psi_f\omega_e = 562$~V equals the peak phase voltage of the
690~V grid.

\section{Wind Profile and Reference Generation}
The wind speed combines a mean value, low-frequency harmonics and a turbulence
term~\cite{regani2026}:
\begin{equation}
v(t) = V_{mean} + \sum_{k=1}^{3} A_k\sin(2\pi f_k t) + v_{turb}(t)
\label{eq:wind}
\end{equation}
with $V_{mean}=8$~m/s, $A=[1.5,\,0.2,\,0.3]$~m/s, $f=[0.03,\,0.16,\,0.17]$~Hz, and $v_{turb}$ a smoothed
Gaussian noise (standard deviation 0.01~m/s, time constant 0.05~s).

The maximum power is extracted at $\lambda=\lambda_{opt}$. The measured wind is filtered,
$\tau_v\dot{\hat v}=v-\hat v$ with $\tau_v=0.3$~s, and the speed reference is obtained from the
critically damped filter
\begin{equation}
\ddot\Omega^* + 2\omega_r\dot\Omega^* + \omega_r^2\Omega^* = \omega_r^2\,\frac{\lambda_{opt}\hat v}{R}
\label{eq:ref}
\end{equation}
with $\omega_r=1.5$~rad/s, which provides $\Omega^*$ and $\dot\Omega^*$ without numerical
differentiation. Since $T_e$ only depends on $i_{sq}$, $i_{sd}^*=0$; unity power factor gives
$i_{gq}^*=0$; the DC-link reference is $V_{dc}^*=1200$~V.

\subsection{Flatness-Based Outer Loops}
A system is flat if its states and inputs can be written as functions of a flat output and of a finite
number of its derivatives~\cite{fliess1995}. The control is then obtained by inverting the model with
a desired dynamics imposed on the flat output. Flatness is used here only for the two outer
subsystems, in which the currents are treated as inputs: the shaft equation (\ref{eq:shaft}) with input
$i_{sq}$, and the power balance of the DC link and filter with input $i_{gd}$. In both, $T_w$ (or its
estimate), $P_{msc}$ and $i_{gq}$ are known exogenous signals. No flatness property is claimed for the
complete sixth-order model with the converter voltages as inputs; the coupling between the outer and
inner loops is treated by Proposition~2.

\emph{Speed loop.} From (\ref{eq:shaft}), $T_e = T_w - B\Omega - J\dot\Omega$, so $y_1=\Omega$ is a flat
output. Replacing $\dot\Omega$ by
\begin{equation}
\nu_1 = \dot\Omega^* + k_{11}e_\Omega + k_{12}\int e_\Omega\,dt,\qquad e_\Omega = \Omega^*-\Omega
\end{equation}
gives
\begin{equation}
T_e^* = \hat T_w - B\Omega - J\nu_1 + \Delta T_e,\qquad i_{sq}^* = \frac{T_e^*}{\frac{3}{2}p\psi_f}
\label{eq:flat1}
\end{equation}
where $\hat T_w$ is computed from (1)--(3) with the measured wind and $\Delta T_e$ is the neural
compensation. $T_e^*$ is limited to $[0,\,1.2T_n]$ and the integral is frozen during saturation. If
$\hat T_w=T_w$ and $\Delta T_e=0$, the error obeys
$\dot e_\Omega + k_{11}e_\Omega + k_{12}\int e_\Omega\,dt = 0$.

\emph{DC-link loop.} The flat output is the energy stored in the DC link and in the filter,
\begin{equation}
y_2 = \frac{1}{2}CV_{dc}^2 + \frac{3}{4}L_f\|\bv{i}_g\|^2 .
\end{equation}
From (\ref{eq:dc}) and (\ref{eq:filter}), using $\bv{i}_g^T\bv{J}\bv{i}_g=0$,
\begin{equation}
\dot y_2 = P_{msc} - \frac{3}{2}v_{gd}i_{gd} - \frac{3}{2}R_f\|\bv{i}_g\|^2
\end{equation}
which is linear in the injected power $P_g=\frac32v_{gd}i_{gd}$, as in~\cite{thounthong2010}. Taking
$y_2$ as the state of this first-order energy balance with input $P_g$, the input is
$P_g=P_{msc}-\frac32R_f\|\bv{i}_g\|^2-\dot y_2$ and $V_{dc}$ follows from $y_2$ and the measured currents,
so $y_2$ is a flat output of the reduced subsystem. With $e_y = y_2^* - y_2$ and
$\dot y_2^*\approx0$, imposing $\dot e_y = -\nu_2$, i.e.\ $\dot y_2 = \nu_2$, with
$\nu_2 = k_{21}e_y + k_{22}\int e_y\,dt$, yields
\begin{equation}
P_g^* = P_{msc} - \frac{3}{2}R_f\|\bv{i}_g\|^2 - \nu_2 + \Delta P_g,\qquad
i_{gd}^* = \frac{2P_g^*}{3v_{gd}}.
\label{eq:flat2}
\end{equation}
Inserting (\ref{eq:flat2}) into the expression of $\dot y_2$ with $i_{gd}=i_{gd}^*$ gives
$\dot y_2 = \nu_2 - \Delta P_g$, hence $\dot e_y + k_{21}e_y + k_{22}\int e_y\,dt = \Delta P_g$.
The term $P_{msc}$, known from the applied voltages and measured currents, anticipates the power
coming from the generator. The reference $y_2^*=\frac12CV_{dc}^{*2}+\frac34L_f\|\bv{i}_g^*\|^2$ varies
only through the filter energy (at most 300~J, against 14.4~kJ in the capacitor), and
$\dot y_2^*=\frac32L_f\bv{i}_g^{*T}\dot{\bv{i}}_g^*$ is neglected. It enters (\ref{eq:erry}) below as an
input, and the impulse response of $s/(s^2+k_{21}s+k_{22})$ has an $L_1$ norm of
$2/(e\,\omega_v)=12.3$~ms, so its effect is $|e_y|\le12.3\,\text{ms}\cdot\sup|\dot y_2^*|$. In simulation,
$|\dot y_2^*|\le2.7$~kW (0.14\,\% of $P_n$) outside the transients, i.e. less than 34~J or 1.4~V on
$V_{dc}$; it reaches 29~kW at start-up and 52~kW during the grid dip. Adding $\dot y_2^*$ to $\nu_2$
reduces the ISE of $V_{dc}$ by 3\,\% but raises the dip deviation from 3.6 to 5.3~V (numerical
differentiation of $\bv{i}_g^*$), so it is omitted. The gains are $k_{11}=2\zeta_\Omega\omega_\Omega$, $k_{12}=\omega_\Omega^2$,
$k_{21}=2\zeta_v\omega_v$, $k_{22}=\omega_v^2$ (Table~\ref{tab:ctrl}).

\subsection{Passivity-Based Current Loops}
For the machine-side converter, the error $\bv{e}_s=\bv{i}_s-\bv{i}_s^*$ and its integral
$\bv{z}_s$ ($\dot{\bv{z}}_s=\bv{e}_s$) are used in
\begin{equation}
\bv{v}_s^* = -L_s\dot{\bv{i}}_s^* - R_s\bv{i}_s^* + \omega_e L_s\bv{J}\bv{i}_s^* + \omega_e\psi_f\bv{e}_q
+ R_a\bv{e}_s + K_i\bv{z}_s
\label{eq:pbc1}
\end{equation}
where $R_a$ is the injected damping. For the grid-side converter, with $\bv{e}_g=\bv{i}_g-\bv{i}_g^*$
and $\dot{\bv{z}}_g=\bv{e}_g$,
\begin{equation}
\bv{v}_i^* = L_f\dot{\bv{i}}_g^* + R_f\bv{i}_g^* - \omega_g L_f\bv{J}\bv{i}_g^* + \bv{v}_g
- R_b\bv{e}_g - K_{ig}\bv{z}_g .
\label{eq:pbc2}
\end{equation}
The reference derivatives are obtained by a first-order filter ($\tau_d=2$~ms) and the voltages are
limited to $V_{dc}/\sqrt{3}$, with the integrals frozen at the limit.

\begin{proposition}
With (\ref{eq:pbc1}) applied to (\ref{eq:pmsg}) and $R_a, K_i>0$, the storage function
$H=\frac{1}{2}L_s\|\bv{e}_s\|^2+\frac{1}{2}K_i\|\bv{z}_s\|^2$ satisfies
$\dot H = -(R_s+R_a)\|\bv{e}_s\|^2\le0$, and $\bv{e}_s\to0$. The same holds for (\ref{eq:pbc2})
applied to (\ref{eq:filter}).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Subtracting $L_s\dot{\bv{i}}_s^*$ from (\ref{eq:pmsg}) and inserting (\ref{eq:pbc1}) gives
$L_s\dot{\bv{e}}_s = -(R_s+R_a)\bv{e}_s + \omega_e L_s\bv{J}\bv{e}_s - K_i\bv{z}_s$. Hence
$\dot H = \bv{e}_s^TL_s\dot{\bv{e}}_s + K_i\bv{z}_s^T\bv{e}_s = -(R_s+R_a)\|\bv{e}_s\|^2
+ \omega_eL_s\bv{e}_s^T\bv{J}\bv{e}_s$, and the last term vanishes because $\bv{J}$ is
skew-symmetric. $H$ is bounded and $\dot H$ is uniformly continuous, so Barbalat's
lemma~\cite{khalil2002} gives $\bv{e}_s\to0$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Adaptive Neural Compensation}
As in~\cite{regani2026}, the network does not replace the nominal control $u_N$ given by
(\ref{eq:flat1})--(\ref{eq:flat2}) and does not modify its gains; it adds
\begin{equation}
u = u_N + u_{AI},\qquad u_{AI} = [\Delta T_e\;\;\Delta P_g]^T .
\end{equation}
Its six inputs are built from the errors
$\bar{\bv{e}} = [\,e_\Omega/(0.01\Omega_n),\; e_y/(300~\text{J}),\; e_{isq}/(0.01I_n)\,]^T$, normalized by
1\,\% of the rated speed, 300~J (about 1\,\% of $V_{dc}^*$) and 1\,\% of the rated current
$I_n=T_n/(\frac32p\psi_f)$, and from their derivatives, filtered with a 5~ms time constant and scaled by
$\boldsymbol{\tau}=[0.5,\,0.02,\,0.002]$~s:
$\bv{z}_n = [\,\bar{\bv{e}}^T,\;(\boldsymbol{\tau}\odot\dot{\bar{\bv{e}}})^T\,]^T$.
With one hidden layer of ten neurons,
\begin{align}
\bv{h} &= \tanh(W_1\bv{z}_n+\bv{b}_1), \qquad \bv{y}=W_2\bv{h}+\bv{b}_2,\\
u_{AI} &= K_{AI}\,\mathrm{diag}(0.1T_n,\,0.1P_n)\,\mathrm{sat}_{\pm3}(\bv{y})
\end{align}
with $W_1\in\mathbb{R}^{10\times6}$ and $W_2\in\mathbb{R}^{2\times10}$.
$T_e$ and $P_g$ enter $\dot e_\Omega$ and $\dot e_y$ with a positive sign, so the learning signal in
the control direction is $\bv{r}=[\bar e_1\;\,\bar e_2]^T$, set to zero inside a dead
zone $\|\bv{r}\|\le\delta$. The parameters are updated online by
\begin{align}
\dot W_2 &= -\Gamma_n\bv{r}\bv{h}^T - \sigma W_2, & \dot{\bv{b}}_2 &= -\Gamma_n\bv{r} - \sigma\bv{b}_2,\\
\dot W_1 &= -\Gamma_n\boldsymbol{\delta}_h\bv{z}_n^T - \sigma W_1, & \dot{\bv{b}}_1 &= -\Gamma_n\boldsymbol{\delta}_h - \sigma\bv{b}_1,
\end{align}
with $\boldsymbol{\delta}_h = (1-\bv{h}^2)\odot(W_2^T\bv{r})$ and the normalized gain
$\Gamma_n = \Gamma/(1+\|\bv{z}_n\|^2)$. The $\sigma$-modification terms~\cite{ioannou1996,lewis1999}
prevent parameter drift, and the normalization~\cite{ioannou1996} slows the adaptation when the errors
are large, e.g.\ after a torque saturation; with a constant gain, a start 10\,\% below the MPPT speed
led to an oscillatory adaptation in simulation. Compared with~\cite{regani2026}, this normalization is
the only change to the update laws. The adaptation is frozen when $T_e^*$ or
$i_{gd}^*$ saturates, and $u_{AI}$ is applied at the next sample. $W_2$ and $\bv{b}_2$ start at zero,
so the compensation is initially inactive.

\begin{proposition}
Consider the model of Section~III-C with (\ref{eq:flat1})--(\ref{eq:pbc2}), $\Delta T_e=\Delta P_g=0$, an exact
model, no saturation, exact reference derivatives, $\dot y_2^*=0$ and $|\omega_e|\le\bar\omega$ on the operating region.
Then the origin of the error state
$\boldsymbol{\xi}=(\bv{e}_s,\bv{z}_s,\bv{e}_g,\bv{z}_g,e_\Omega,\!\int\!e_\Omega,e_y,\!\int\!e_y)$ is exponentially stable.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
\emph{Inner loops.} With $V_s = H + \varepsilon\,\bv{e}_s^TL_s\bv{z}_s$,
$\dot V_s = -(R_s+R_a)\|\bv{e}_s\|^2 + \varepsilon\bigl(L_s\|\bv{e}_s\|^2 - (R_s+R_a)\bv{e}_s^T\bv{z}_s
- \omega_eL_s\bv{e}_s^T\bv{J}\bv{z}_s - K_i\|\bv{z}_s\|^2\bigr)$. Since $|\omega_e|\le\bar\omega$, a
small $\varepsilon>0$ makes $V_s$ positive definite and $\dot V_s\le-c(\|\bv{e}_s\|^2+\|\bv{z}_s\|^2)$
with $c>0$, so $(\bv{e}_s,\bv{z}_s)\to0$ exponentially; the same holds for $(\bv{e}_g,\bv{z}_g)$
since $\omega_g$ is constant.
\emph{Outer loops.} With the actual currents, $T_e = T_e^* + \frac32p\psi_f e_{sq}$ and
$i_{gd} = i_{gd}^* + e_{gd}$, so (\ref{eq:shaft}) and the $y_2$ dynamics give
\begin{align*}
\dot e_\Omega + k_{11}e_\Omega + k_{12}\!\int\! e_\Omega &= \tfrac{3p\psi_f}{2J}\,e_{sq},\\
\dot e_y + k_{21}e_y + k_{22}\!\int\! e_y &= \tfrac32 v_{gd}\,e_{gd}.
\end{align*}
These are exponentially stable linear systems ($k_{ij}>0$) driven by exponentially decaying inputs
with bounded gains. The inner error dynamics do not depend on the outer states, so the
interconnection is a cascade of exponentially stable systems and is exponentially
stable~\cite{khalil2002}.
\end{proof}
The boundedness proof of~\cite{regani2026} uses a Lyapunov function with
$\mathrm{tr}(\tilde W^T\Gamma^{-1}\tilde W)$ and a \emph{constant} gain; with $\Gamma_n(t)$ an additional
term in $\dot\Gamma_n$ appears, so it does not apply directly, but the normalization gives a direct
bound. The model error $\Delta=T_w-\hat T_w$ and the compensation enter the outer error equations of
Proposition~2 as additional inputs:
\begin{align}
\dot e_\Omega + k_{11}e_\Omega + k_{12}\!\int\! e_\Omega &= \tfrac{3p\psi_f}{2J}\,e_{sq}
+ \tfrac{1}{J}(\Delta T_e-\Delta),\label{eq:errw}\\
\dot e_y + k_{21}e_y + k_{22}\!\int\! e_y &= \tfrac32 v_{gd}\,e_{gd} + \Delta P_g + \dot y_2^* .
\label{eq:erry}
\end{align}

\begin{proposition}
Let $\Gamma,\sigma>0$. (i) Whatever the tracking errors, the parameters of the network remain bounded:
with Frobenius norms,
$\|W_2(t)\|\le\bar W_2:=\max\bigl(\|W_2(0)\|,\sqrt{10}\,\Gamma/(2\sigma)\bigr)$,
$\|\bv{b}_2(t)\|\le\max\bigl(\|\bv{b}_2(0)\|,\Gamma/(2\sigma)\bigr)$,
$\|W_1(t)\|\le\max\bigl(\|W_1(0)\|,\Gamma\bar W_2/\sigma\bigr)$ and
$\|\bv{b}_1(t)\|\le\max\bigl(\|\bv{b}_1(0)\|,\Gamma\bar W_2/(2\sigma)\bigr)$.
(ii) Under the assumptions of Proposition~2, except $\Delta T_e=\Delta P_g=0$, and for bounded $\Delta$
and $\dot y_2^*$, the error state $\boldsymbol\xi$ is uniformly ultimately bounded, with an ultimate
bound proportional to $\sup_t\bigl(|\Delta T_e-\Delta|/J+|\Delta P_g+\dot y_2^*|\bigr)$, as long as the
trajectory remains in the operating region of Proposition~2.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
(i) The learning signal $\bv{r}$ is zero or equal to the first two entries of $\bv{z}_n$, so
$\|\bv{r}\|\le\|\bv{z}_n\|$, and $\|\bv{h}\|\le\sqrt{10}$. Hence
$\Gamma_n\|\bv{r}\|\|\bv{h}\|\le\sqrt{10}\,\Gamma\|\bv{z}_n\|/(1+\|\bv{z}_n\|^2)\le\sqrt{10}\,\Gamma/2$,
and $\frac{d}{dt}\frac12\|W_2\|^2\le-\sigma\|W_2\|^2+\frac{\sqrt{10}}{2}\Gamma\|W_2\|$, which gives the bound
on $W_2$; that on $\bv{b}_2$ follows in the same way. Then
$\|\boldsymbol\delta_h\|\le\bar W_2\|\bv{r}\|$ and
$\Gamma_n\|\boldsymbol\delta_h\|\|\bv{z}_n\|\le\Gamma\bar W_2\|\bv{z}_n\|^2/(1+\|\bv{z}_n\|^2)\le\Gamma\bar W_2$,
which bounds $W_1$ and $\bv{b}_1$. Freezing the adaptation keeps the parameters constant.
(ii) $u_{AI}$ is bounded by its saturation ($\pm0.3T_n$, $\pm0.3P_n$). The error system of
Proposition~2 is exponentially stable and the inputs of (\ref{eq:errw})--(\ref{eq:erry}) enter it
linearly, so it is locally input-to-state stable~\cite{khalil2002}, which gives the bound.
\end{proof}

Proposition~3 holds for any $\Gamma>0$, independently of the approximation capability of the
network, and does not rely on~\cite{regani2026}. It does not prove that $\Delta T_e$ approaches
$\Delta$, i.e., that the network reduces the bound; this is assessed in Section~VI.

The bounds grow with $\Gamma/\sigma$: in simulation, $\Gamma\ge20$ saturates the network output and the
DC-link loop oscillates with a bounded amplitude; $\Gamma=5$ is used.

\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{nominal}: the controller uses the true parameters;
\item \emph{robustness}: the controller keeps the nominal values while the plant has
$R_s\times1.5$, $L_s\times1.2$, $\psi_f\times0.92$, $R_f\times1.5$, $L_f\times1.25$; $\hat T_w$ is

\subsection{Comparison with PI Vector Control}
Table~\ref{tab:res} and Figs.~\ref{fig:tracking}--\ref{fig:cmp} summarize the results.
\emph{Speed:} since the flat law contains $J\dot\Omega^*$ and $\hat T_w$, the IAE is 3.8 times
(robustness) to $10^4$ times (nominal) smaller than with PI, and the energy is 0.5--0.6\,\% higher.
\emph{DC link:} the feedforward of $P_{msc}$ is decisive (PI without it gives a 200--300 times larger
ISE); during the dip, the deviation is 3.6~V, against 11.6~V with PI+FF and 62~V with PI. PI+FF keeps
a smaller steady ripple (0.03 vs 0.36~V) and start-up deviation (3.9 vs 10.4~V).
\emph{Currents:} PI tracks them better (RMS error of $i_{sq}$ 0.84 vs 3.07~A under mismatch), because
the PBC feedforward uses nominal parameters; all errors remain below 0.3\,\% of the rated current.

\subsection{Effect of the Neural Compensation}
Fig.~\ref{fig:ann} shows that, without knowing the 10\,\% model error, the network converges in less
than one second to $\Delta T_e\approx0.065$~MN$\cdot$m, close to the mean of the actual error
$T_w-\hat T_w$ (0.047 to 0.077~MN$\cdot$m); the slowly varying part is handled by the integral action
of (\ref{eq:flat1}). The IAE of the speed decreases by 38\,\%, mainly during the start-up transient,
where the peak speed error drops from 6.6 to 5.2~mrad/s. Table~\ref{tab:ann} reports additional tests.

Over five turbulence realizations, the IAE is reduced by $38\pm0.1$\,\% and the ISE of $V_{dc}$ by

and the DC link rises by over 120~V, which requires a braking chopper or pitch control.

\subsection{Switched Model and Computational Cost}
A switched model of the two-level converters (space-vector modulation, 2160~Hz carrier, double update,
1~$\mu$s step) was simulated at 8, 9 and 10~m/s with nominal parameters. The phase-$a$ grid current is
analyzed by FFT over ten periods in steady state; as in IEEE~519~\cite{ieee519}, the harmonics
$h=2,\dots,50$ are referred to the rated current $I_L$ at the converter terminals. The total demand
distortion (TDD) is 0.12, 0.07 and 0.13\,\%, and the largest harmonic 0.11\,\% of $I_L$, below the
limits (5\,\% TDD, 0.3\,\% for $35\le h\le50$ in the strictest class). The switching components are
not covered by these indices: since $f_{sw}/f_g=43.2$, they are interharmonics, mainly at
$f_{sw}\pm2f_g$ and $f_{sw}\pm4f_g$ (2.2 and 1.6\,\% of $I_L$ each) and near $2f_{sw}$, about 4.6\,\% of
$I_L$ in total up to 5~kHz, nearly independent of the load. With a grid-synchronized carrier they would
exceed the 0.3\,\% limit, so an $LCL$ filter is required at this switching frequency. PI control gives a
TDD of 0.09--0.11\,\% and the same switching components within 0.1\,\% of $I_L$: this ripple is set by
the modulation and the filter, not by the control.

Per sample, the network needs about 200 multiply--accumulate operations and ten $\tanh$ evaluations,
and the flat and PBC laws fewer than 100 operations, i.e., a few MFLOP/s at 10~kHz, within the
capability of current digital signal processors.

\section{Conclusion}
A complete model of a 2~MW direct-drive PMSG wind energy conversion system was developed with machine
data from the literature. Its control combines flatness for the speed and DC-link energy loops,
passivity-based current control for both converters, with a cascade stability proof, and a normalized
adaptive neural compensation with guaranteed bounded parameters. Compared with PI vector control tuned
on the same poles, it tracks the MPPT speed 3.8 times more accurately under model errors and limits the
DC-link deviation during a 20\,\% grid dip to 3.6~V (11.6~V with PI and feedforward), at the price of
larger current errors. The network reduces the speed error by 38\,\%. Future work will address a
braking chopper and pitch control, an $LCL$ filter, saturations in the stability analysis, and
experimental validation.
